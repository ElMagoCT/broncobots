FRC Team 991

The flagship. A new game in January, six weeks to build, then competition. Six subteams, every one of them student-led.

Six subteams, one mechanism

A three-stage elevator is a good picture of how 991 works: nothing moves unless every subteam did its part, and the whole thing is judged on the last four inches.

  1. Mechanical

    Stages, bearings and tolerances. Designed in Onshape, machined in the shop.

  2. Electrical

    Motor controllers and limit switches, on a harness that survives being hit.

  3. Software

    Closed-loop height control, so the carriage stops where the driver asked.

  4. Drive team

    Place the piece under pressure, in the last thirty seconds, on the first try.

How the team is organised

Student-led subteams

A student can go deep on one discipline while still watching the whole machine come together. Every subteam has a mentor available and none of them is run by an adult.

Season rhythm

  1. Kickoff weekend

    The game is revealed. The team reads the manual together, then argues about scoring priorities.

  2. Weeks 1–2 — design

    Prototyping, field mockups, full CAD. Decisions lock so fabrication can start.

  3. Weeks 3–5 — build

    Machining, assembly, wiring, first code. The longest days of the year.

  4. Week 6 — practice

    Driver practice and failure testing. Break it now, not at competition.

  5. Competition

    Arizona regionals, then championship play if we earn it.
