A match is 150 seconds

If you have never seen one, this is the whole thing. Two robots form an alliance against two others, and your son’s partner changes almost every match.

  1. Autonomous — 0:30

    The robot runs entirely on code written beforehand. Nobody may touch a controller. Points here are worth the most per second.

  2. Driver-controlled — 2:00

    Two students take over: one driving, one operating mechanisms. Scoring, defence and cycle speed.

  3. Endgame — final 0:30

    Higher-value scoring opens up. Most matches are decided here, and most robots break here.

The sixty-second version

FIRST Tech Challenge — everyone says “FTC” — is a varsity sport for engineering. Each fall a new game is announced. A team has a few months to design, build and program a robot roughly the size of a large microwave, then competes head-to-head against other schools in front of a crowd, a referee and a scoreboard.

Your son will not be handed a kit and told to follow instructions. He will be handed a problem nobody has solved yet, a team of peers, a deadline he cannot move, and a shop full of tools.

That is the part parents usually do not expect: this program is run by the students. Adults are moderators and mentors. They teach, supervise, drive the van and keep everyone safe. They do not build the robot.

Not sure which program he joined?

FTC has five teams and is where most students start. FRC is the larger flagship team (991) with a bigger robot and a spring season. FLL is the LEGO-based program our students mentor. Compare all three ›

The phrase you will hear all season: Gracious Professionalism™

It is enforced, not decorative. Teams lend each other parts mid-tournament and will help repair the robot about to play against them. Referees and judges genuinely score it — and it is the thing parents most often say surprised them at their first event.

The shape of a year

From your kitchen calendar

The intensity is not flat. Knowing which weeks are which makes the whole thing easier.

  • August

    Preseason

    Interest meetings, safety and tool training, CAD and programming bootcamps. The month to try it with no strings attached.

  • September

    Kickoff & design

    The new game drops. Teams prototype badly and throw a lot of it away. Two or three afternoons a week.

  • Oct – Nov

    Build & first competitions

    The busiest stretch. Saturdays and some later evenings.

  • December

    Iterate over break

    Rebuild the weak subsystem. The shop is open on a posted schedule, not every day.

  • Jan – Feb

    Championship push

    League finals and, for teams that advance, the state championship. FRC build season starts too.

  • Mar – May

    Offseason

    Demos, outreach, FLL mentoring, and handing leadership to next year’s students.

The honest answer

How much time is this really?

Robotics is the rare activity where a student can genuinely dial the commitment up or down. These are the three tiers we actually see.

LevelWeeklyWhat it looks like
Member3–5 hrsOne or two afternoons, occasional Saturday, attends competitions as pit or scouting crew. For students with a full plate.
Core builder6–10 hrsTwo or three afternoons plus most Saturdays in season. Owns a subsystem or a piece of the code. The most common tier.
Lead12+ hrsRuns a subteam, manages the schedule, writes the notebook, mentors rookies, presents to judges. A leadership job.

Peak weeks are real

Before a tournament and during the six-week FRC build, expect late afternoons and full Saturdays. Always published in advance on the calendar.

Academics come first

Grades are the condition of participation. Moderators will send a student to study hall over the shop. Tell us early if he is struggling.

Your first tournament

  1. Load-in & inspection

    Pit setup and safety inspection. Students often arrive before 7 a.m.

  2. Judging interviews

    Students present their season to a panel. Parents do not attend this part.

  3. Qualification matches

    Several matches with rotating partners, repairs in between. Most of the day.

  4. Alliance selection

    Top teams pick partners. Genuinely tense, and the best moment to watch.

  5. Eliminations & awards

    Bracket play, then the ceremony where advancement is decided.

Questions we get every August

Frequently asked

My son has zero experience. Is he behind?

No. Roughly half of every incoming class has never used a power tool or written code. The program is built to teach from zero, and the game resets every year anyway — nobody starts with a finished answer.

Can he do robotics and a sport?

Yes, and many do. Tell the moderators up front so he lands in a role that fits — scouting, programming and business roles are especially flexible. A fall sport does overlap the busiest part of the FTC season, so his contribution will look different. That is fine.

Is this only for future engineers?

No. The team needs writers, photographers, presenters, business students and strategists as much as machinists. The judged awards reward exactly those skills.

How do I know what is happening this week?
  • The team calendar — every meeting and competition.
  • The weekly bulletin — a short Monday summary.
  • The email list — add your address for schedule changes.
FTC versus FRC again?

Scale. FTC robots start inside an 18-inch cube and run a fall season with five small teams. FRC robots are vending-machine sized with a January–April season and one large team (991). Many students do both. Full comparison ›

Does he need his own laptop or tools?

No. The team provides the robot, control system, tools and shop. Students use school or team laptops. Personal safety glasses are welcome but we keep a supply.

Is there travel?

Local qualifiers are around the Phoenix metro — day trips, students travel together. State stays in Arizona. Anything beyond is rare, announced far ahead, and comes with a fundraising plan. Parents are never required to travel.

How do I reach a real person?

Email broncobots@brophyprep.org — placeholder address, confirm before publishing — or use the contact form. We would rather hear a small question in September than a big one in February.
