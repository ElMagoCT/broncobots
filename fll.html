Teams / FLL

FIRST LEGO League

The one program where we do not compete. Our students coach Loyola Academy scholars — and learn to teach in the process.

Mentorship, not competition

We are the coaches here

Broncobot members guide Loyola Academy scholars in coding, design and teamwork, helping them discover the creativity of robotics through hands-on challenges.

Our students plan the sessions, teach the build, debug someone else’s code without taking the keyboard, and stand behind the table at the tournament while somebody younger drives. Most of them say it is the hardest and best thing they do here.

What our mentors actually do

Teaching is the skill

📚

Plan the season

Break the challenge into weekly sessions so an eleven-year-old gets from zero to a working robot.

🧠

Teach block coding

Start with blocks, then introduce loops, sensors and conditionals as the missions demand them.

✋

Keep hands off

The hardest rule: let them build it wrong, then ask the question that helps them find it.

💬

Coach the pitch

FLL teams present a research project to judges. Our students help them practise speaking to adults.

🎉

Show up on event day

Broncobots attend in team colours to cheer, and volunteer wherever the event needs hands.

👥

Recruit the next class

Many Loyola scholars who do FLL arrive at Brophy already knowing they want this team.

What FLL is, briefly

Teams build and program a LEGO-based robot to run missions on a themed tabletop field, and also research a real-world problem tied to that season’s theme. It is judged on the robot, the project and core values — and the core values matter most.

Mentors needed

Want to coach a squad?

Any Broncobot can mentor. It runs in the fall alongside the FTC season and takes about two hours a week. Tell us and we will place you.
