Programs / FTC

FIRST Tech Challenge

Five student-led teams, five robots, one shop. FTC is where most Broncobots start — and where a single student can own an entire machine.

About the program

Small teams, total ownership

With five student-led teams, FTC offers our members the chance to innovate, compete, and collaborate on a smaller scale. Each team designs, builds, and programs its own robot for regional and state competitions, learning to balance independence with mentorship and teamwork.

Every robot has to start each match fitting inside an 18-inch cube, run on the approved control system, and pass inspection. Inside those limits, everything is the team's call. With eight to twelve students per team, there is nowhere to hide and no subsystem that somebody is not personally responsible for — which is exactly why rookies learn fast here.

Teams compete at league meets and qualifying tournaments around the Phoenix metro area through the fall, with the Arizona State Championship in the winter.

How a match works

Two and a half minutes

Two robots form an alliance against two others. Your partner changes almost every match, so strategy and communication matter as much as the machine.

  1. Autonomous — 0:30

    The robot runs entirely on pre-written code. No driver input allowed. Points here are worth the most per second of the match.

  2. Driver-controlled — 2:00

    Two students per robot take over: one driving, one operating the mechanisms. Scoring, defense, and cycle speed.

  3. Endgame — final 0:30

    Higher-value scoring opportunities open up. Most matches are decided here, and most robots break here.

The judged awards matter

The Inspire Award — FTC's top honor — goes to the strongest all-around team, not the highest scorer. The engineering notebook, the judge interview, and outreach can send a team to the state championship on their own. More in the parent guide ›
