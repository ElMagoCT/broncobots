Our History

From one team in a borrowed classroom to six teams, a dedicated shop, and a mentoring program — the short version.

The through-line

Built by students, handed down

Every year this program is rebuilt by a new group of students, and every year they inherit the shop, the tooling, and the habits of the class before them.

The Broncobots began as a single FIRST Robotics Competition team and grew as demand outran capacity — first by adding FTC teams so more students could own a robot, then by adding FLL mentoring so the program could give something back to younger students in the Phoenix area.

How we got here

  • Founding

    FRC 991 is chartered

    Brophy enters the FIRST Robotics Competition with a small group of students, a borrowed classroom, and donated tools. Placeholder — confirm the founding year.

  • Early years

    First regional competition

    The team travels to its first Arizona regional. Nothing works the way it did in the shop, which is the lesson everyone learns in year one.

  • Growth

    FTC teams 201 and 202 are formed

    Demand exceeds what one FRC team can absorb. Two FTC teams launch so more students can own an entire robot rather than a corner of one.

  • Expansion

    A dedicated robotics shop

    The program moves into a permanent space with machining tools, a full practice field, and room for multiple teams to build at once.

  • Outreach

    FLL mentoring at Loyola Academy begins

    Broncobots start coaching Loyola Academy scholars through FIRST LEGO League, turning the program's students into teachers.

  • Recent

    Five FTC teams

    23737, 26983, and 30596 join 201 and 202. The organization reaches six competition teams across two programs.
