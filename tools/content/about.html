Mission

Team mission & dynamics

We are dedicated to educating students in the engineering and design of robots. We accept all students regardless of previous experience and work to educate and give back to the surrounding community.

By creating an atmosphere of Gracious Professionalism™, the Broncobots develop students who care for and recognize the God-given talents in one another. We give those members a chance to explore themselves and learn and grow as people so that they can go out and make a difference in the community.

At the heart of the Broncobots is collaboration. Each member contributes unique skills that come together to form one strong, cohesive team. We believe in learning by doing, helping one another grow, and pushing the limits of what students can achieve when given the tools and trust to lead.

Our creed

Trust. Respect. Commitment.

Three words on the shop wall. They are how we decide things when the rules do not say.

🤝

Trust

Students are handed real budgets, real tools, and real decisions. Trust is why this program is student-led rather than adult-run — and it is earned by showing up and following through.

☸

Respect

For teammates, for competitors, for the mentors who give up their evenings, and for the tools and the shop. Gracious Professionalism™ is respect with a scoreboard attached.

🔨

Commitment

Deadlines do not move. A robot that is 90 percent finished on competition morning does not compete. Commitment is the habit this program builds most reliably.

How the team runs

Student-led, mentor-supported

Students own the work

Students set the build schedule, choose the design, manage the budget, write the code, present to judges, and lead the subteams. Team captains and subteam leads are students, selected each spring for the following season.

Mentors teach and supervise

Brophy faculty moderators and volunteer mentors run safety and tool training, supervise the shop, drive to events, and answer questions. They do not build the robot. That distinction is the whole program.

Everyone is accepted

No tryouts to join, no experience required, and no cut. The August bootcamps exist precisely so a student with zero background can contribute by September.

We give the program away

Mentoring Loyola Academy FLL teams, running demos, and helping other teams at competition are all part of the job, not extras. See the FLL program ›
