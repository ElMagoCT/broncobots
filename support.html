Sponsors & Mentors

Robotics is an expensive sport to run and an inexpensive one to join. Here is exactly where support goes — and why skills help as much as money.

Where support goes

Follow the current

Every dollar and every volunteer hour ends up powering one of four things. Nothing here funds a trophy case — it funds students doing the work.

  • Registration & event fees — six teams, multiple events, paid before the season starts.
  • Materials & spare parts — aluminium, motors, controllers, and the second one you need when the first breaks.
  • Tools & shop equipment — the machines students are trained on and use for years.
  • Outreach & FLL mentoring — kits and event costs for the Loyola Academy scholars we coach.

Cost is never a barrier to a student

If dues, a team shirt or a travel event would be a hardship for a family, it is handled quietly and it happens every year. Sponsorship is part of how that stays true.

Matching gifts

Many employers match charitable giving. If yours does, it is the single easiest way to double your support — ask your HR team about a matching-gift program.

In-kind is real support

Aluminium stock, machining time, a spare laptop, printing, or a truck on a competition Saturday all count and are all genuinely needed.

Mentors

You do not need an engineering degree

The most useful volunteers this program has had were a nurse, an accountant and somebody who owned a truck. Mentors teach a skill; they do not build the robot.
